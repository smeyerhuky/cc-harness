# Thermoforming

Thermoforming shapes a flat thermoplastic sheet into a 3D part. It is commonly used for packaging, trays, and large panels.

## Mechanism
1. **Heating**: A plastic sheet is clamped and heated (typically via infrared heaters) to its forming temperature (above Tg but below Tm).
2. **Forming**: The softened sheet is stretched over or into a temperature-controlled mold. Vacuum, air pressure, or mechanical plugs are used to conform the sheet to the mold [geometry](../mathematics/geometry.md).
3. **Cooling**: The part cools against the mold until rigid.
4. **Trimming**: The part is removed and the excess material (web) is trimmed.

## Physics & Constraints
* **Draw Ratio**: The depth of draw is limited. Deep draws cause excessive thinning. The area draw ratio is the ratio of the formed surface area to the original sheet area.
* **Thickness Variation**: Material thins out at corners and deep sections. Plug-assist forming helps distribute material more uniformly.
* **Tooling Cost**: Tooling is significantly cheaper than [injection molding](../processes/injection-molding.md) because pressures are much lower, allowing the use of aluminum, wood, or epoxy molds.
