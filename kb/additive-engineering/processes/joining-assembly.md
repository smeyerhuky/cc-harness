# Joining and Assembly

In traditional manufacturing, complex products are rarely fabricated as a single continuous piece. Instead, they are broken down into simpler, easier-to-manufacture components that must subsequently be joined together. This stage is known as Assembly.

## Common Joining Processes

Joining processes generally fall into three categories:

1. **Mechanical Fastening**: 
   Using hardware like bolts, screws, nuts, and rivets to hold parts together. Mechanical fastening is often preferred when the assembly needs to be disassembled for maintenance or repair. It requires precise tolerancing and matching geometry.

2. **Welding and Fusion**:
   Fusing materials (typically metals or thermoplastics) by melting the interfaces and allowing them to solidify together, often with a filler material. This creates a permanent, high-strength joint but can introduce thermal stresses and distortion.

3. **Adhesive Bonding**:
   Using chemical adhesives (glues, epoxies) to bond surfaces. Adhesives distribute stress evenly across a joint (unlike point fasteners) and can join dissimilar materials, but they may require significant curing time and surface preparation.

## Assembly Constraints and Costs

Assembly adds significant complexity and cost to the manufacturing lifecycle:
* **Tolerancing**: Individual components must be manufactured with precise tolerances to ensure they fit together correctly during assembly.
* **Labor and Automation Costs**: Assembly often requires manual labor or expensive robotic automation.
* **Inventory**: Managing multiple discrete parts increases supply chain and inventory complexity.

## Additive Manufacturing and Part Consolidation

One of the primary advantages of [Layered and Additive Manufacturing](layered-manufacturing.md) is the ability to consolidate multiple sub-components into a single, complex design. **Part Consolidation** eliminates the need for separate joining processes, reduces assembly time, removes potential points of failure at joints, and lowers the overall weight and inventory overhead.
