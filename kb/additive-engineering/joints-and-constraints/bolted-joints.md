# Bolted Joints

Bolted joints are highly preloaded semi-permanent connections.

## Preload and Load Sharing
When a bolted joint is subjected to an external tensile load $P$:
- The bolt and the clamped members act as springs in **parallel**.
- Because the clamped members are much stiffer ($k_m \gg k_b$) than the bolt, the members absorb the vast majority of the external load (typically >85%).
- The load carried by the bolt is $P_b = C \cdot P$, where $C = \frac{k_b}{k_m + k_b}$.

## Shear Loads
Bolts should **never** be loaded in shear. Shear forces should be resisted entirely by the **friction** between the clamped members, which is generated by the bolt's tensile preload ($F = \mu_s F_{preload}$). For high shear applications, use dowel pins or shoulder bolts.

## Torque vs. Preload
Tightening torque is mostly lost to friction:
- ~50% overcomes bolt head friction.
- ~40% overcomes thread friction.
- Only ~10% actually stretches the bolt to create preload.
