# Core Manufacturing Attributes

Manufacturing systems and unit processes are evaluated and optimized against four fundamental attributes:

1. **Rate (Production Rate)**: The speed at which parts are produced (e.g., parts per hour). It is constrained by physical limits (cooling time in molding, heat dissipation in cutting) and system bottlenecks.
2. **Cost**: Total expenditure required to produce a part. It includes fixed costs (tooling, machinery) and variable costs (material, labor, energy). The per-part cost decreases with volume due to the amortization of fixed costs.
3. **Quality**: The degree to which a part meets its specifications (dimensional accuracy, surface finish, material properties). Quality is inversely proportional to variation.
4. **Flexibility**: The ability of a manufacturing system to adapt to changes in product design, volume, or type without significant penalty in time or cost.

These attributes are deeply interconnected. For instance, increasing the rate often negatively impacts quality or increases cost (due to requiring more expensive, faster machinery). Optimizing a process involves balancing these attributes against the product's market requirements.
